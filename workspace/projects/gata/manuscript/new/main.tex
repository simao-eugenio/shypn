% ── Abstract ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\section*{Abstract}

Whether cytokines instruct haematopoietic lineage fate or merely bias a
stochastic choice has remained contested for three decades. Bulk colony assays
consistently produced sharp dose-response curves implying that cytokine
concentration deterministically switches erythroid commitment on or off, while
single-cell imaging of the same progenitors revealed substantial fate
heterogeneity under identical exposure. We show that both observations are
simultaneously correct: the apparent contradiction dissolves once molecular copy
number and the ratio of competing cytokine signals are both made explicit.

Using Signal Hierarchical Petri Nets~\cite{shypn2026}, we modelled the
GATA1/PU.1 fate switch at single-cell copy numbers and performed complementary
cytokine sweeps totalling more than three thousand stochastic replicates. At
physiological single-cell copy numbers, erythroid commitment probability was
statistically flat across a wide EPO range---stochastic noise in the decision
layer, not cytokine concentration, is the proximate determinant of individual
fate. The true bifurcation is controlled by the ratio of GCSF to EPO receptor
occupancy: commitment probability exceeds ninety percent below the threshold and
collapses to zero above it, with an abrupt switch in between. A three-layer
cascade architecture explains why EPO dose amplification cannot cross this
boundary: the reception layer sets a fixed receptor stoichiometry, the decision
layer is where noise-driven fate arises, and the execution layer fires
EPO-independently once triggered.

A cytokine-withdrawal experiment demonstrates that the erythroid state is
maintained by ongoing receptor signalling, not by an epigenetic lock---a
distinction with direct implications for the interpretation of commitment timing
in live-cell imaging studies. A systematic pulse-duration sweep reveals a
non-monotone relationship between EPO exposure time and fate maintenance: stable
post-withdrawal erythroid commitment is achievable only within a narrow,
bracketed pulse window, with both shorter and longer exposures reverting to the
myeloid attractor by distinct mechanisms.

The largest set of results reported here is a factorial EPO--pH sweep spanning
2400 stochastic replicates across eight EPO concentrations and three nuclear pH
values. Nuclear pH shifts the EPO commitment threshold in a direction and by a
magnitude that net-flux analysis alone cannot predict, and a single EPO
concentration near the bifurcation produces opposite majority fates at acidic
versus alkaline pH. More than half of the conditions tested showed genuine
stochastic bistability by quantitative bimodality criterion. These results
generate five falsifiable experimental predictions, including a precisely
bounded EPO pulse-duration window and a clonal copy-number assay to demonstrate
directly that the classical commitment threshold is a population-averaging
artefact.

% ── Author Summary ────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\section*{Author Summary}

In the bone marrow, immature blood cells must decide whether to become red
blood cells or white blood cells. For thirty years, scientists have debated
whether the hormone EPO simply orders a cell to become red, or whether the
cell makes that choice randomly and EPO only changes the odds. The debate
persisted because experiments with many cells in a dish told one story and
single-cell experiments told another. In this work we built a computer
simulation of a single progenitor cell that tracks individual molecules of
the key proteins GATA1 and PU.1, the two master regulators that control this
decision. When we ran the simulation as two thousand four hundred independent
virtual cells under the same conditions, we found that beyond fifty percent of
the tested conditions showed genuine bistability: the same inputs produced two
distinct stable outcomes depending on early molecular fluctuations, not on
cytokine dose. We also found that nuclear pH---an environmental parameter
rarely considered in EPO signalling studies---shifts the EPO dose required for
the majority of cells to commit to the erythroid fate, and that a single EPO
concentration near the switching point can drive opposite majority fates
depending solely on pH. Both prior bodies of experimental evidence are therefore
correct: averaged over many cells the system looks instructive, but one cell at
a time it is stochastic. Our simulation framework, Signal Hierarchical Petri
Nets, is described in full in the final section of this paper as a reusable
resource for modelling other hybrid biological switching circuits.

are publicly available at
\url{https://github.com/simao-eugenio/shypn}~\cite{shypn2026}
(DOI:~\href{https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.18749556}{10.5281/zenodo.18749556})
