We present Signal Hierarchical Petri Nets (SHYPN), a computational platform extending classical Petri net formalism with signal places and specialized arc semantics for biosystems modeling. The platform addresses the challenge of representing information flow distinct from mass transfer by formalizing signal sensing mechanisms that enable non-local place references without violating mass conservation. We validate capabilities using the MAPK cascade, reproducing published adaptation behavior where ERK activity returns to baseline despite sustained stimulus.

The platform introduces specialized arc types: test arcs for non-consumptive catalytic modulation, and signal_flow arcs for hierarchical preemption. Signal places hold tokens representing regulatory information with temporal dynamics distinct from mass-transfer kinetics. This separation enables long-range feedback representation without creating physical cycles. Signal type classification (ENERGY, SPATIAL, QUORUM, REGULATORY) organizes hierarchical control across four semantic layers.

Validation demonstrates systematic parameter space exploration. The model reproduces dual-pathway negative feedback where MEK-PP provides fast proportional feedback (2 minute response) while ERK-PP establishes slow integral control (60 minute accumulation). Their combination achieves 96.5% adaptation quality with 95% flux suppression. Progressive thermodynamic validation modes (classical, ATP-coupled, comprehensive) ensure physical plausibility.

The platform provides infrastructure for exploring hierarchical control in signal transduction networks. Bidirectional SBML compliance enables interoperability with curated databases. The framework demonstrates how formalizing information flow within Petri net formalism enables rigorous regulatory analysis while preserving structural capabilities.
