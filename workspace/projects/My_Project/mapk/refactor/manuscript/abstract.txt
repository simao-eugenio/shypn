We extend classical Petri net formalism with signal hierarchy theory, introducing a 13-tuple framework that formalizes information flow in biochemical networks. This theoretical extension addresses the challenge of representing regulatory feedback distinct from mass transfer by introducing signal places with specialized arc semantics that enable non-local references without violating mass conservation principles. Mathematical validation through the mitogen-activated protein kinase (MAPK) cascade demonstrates the formalism's capacity to prove adaptation conditions where ERK activity returns to baseline despite sustained stimulus.

The formalism introduces arc type classification (normal, test, signal_flow, inhibitor) that distinguishes consumptive mass transfer from non-consumptive information sensing. Signal places hold tokens with temporal dynamics separate from stoichiometric reactions, enabling long-range feedback representation without creating physical cycles. Signal type hierarchy (ENERGY, SPATIAL, QUORUM, REGULATORY) organizes control across four semantic layers (Layer 0 through Layer 3), formalizing hierarchical decision-making observed in biological regulation.

Mathematical analysis of MAPK dual-feedback architecture demonstrates that multiplicative signal combination achieves 96.5% adaptation quality through hierarchical preemption. Fast MEK-PP proportional feedback (2-minute timescale) combines with slow ERK-PP integral control (60-minute accumulation), with 95% suppression of forward flux at equilibrium. Thermodynamic validation via ATP-coupled equilibrium constants ensures physical plausibility across explored parameter space.

This theoretical framework provides mathematical infrastructure for rigorous analysis of hierarchical control architectures in signal transduction networks. The formalism demonstrates how extending Petri net theory with signal semantics enables formal analysis of regulatory mechanisms while preserving structural properties for topology analysis and thermodynamic validation.
