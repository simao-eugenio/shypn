\section*{Abstract}
Predicting cellular commitment thresholds---such as ATP concentrations triggering irreversible developmental transitions---requires modeling regulatory signal consumption during decision-making. Biological Petri net theory lacks formal semantics for such hierarchical regulatory signals. Classical test arcs model catalysis (non-consuming reads) but cannot express signal consumption essential for commitment finality and pathway preemption. We introduce \textit{signal hierarchy theory}, a formal extension to Bio-PN providing signal places as information channels distinct from metabolic mass transfer, with signal flow arcs enabling consumption semantics. Hierarchical layer assignment formalizes multi-scale organization where higher regulatory layers control lower metabolic layers through regulatory consumption. The formalism bridges the expressiveness gap between metabolic mass transfer (normal arcs) and regulatory information flow (signal flow arcs), enabling unified modeling where energy status functions simultaneously as metabolite and regulatory signal. We provide complete formal definitions of the 13-tuple extension including signal places ($\Psi$), signal flow arc semantics, hierarchical layer functions, and enablement thresholds. We demonstrate the formalism's applicability through \textit{Bacillus subtilis} sporulation, an illustrative example: signal hierarchy analysis computes ATP commitment threshold at 2.38 mM, matching experimental measurements (2.21 mM, 7\% error)---a prediction inexpressible in classical Bio-PN lacking consumption semantics. This proof-of-concept demonstrates how the formalism enables quantitative threshold determination, basin geometry quantification, and commitment reliability analysis, providing theoretical foundation for modeling irreversible cellular decisions under energy constraints.

\section*{Author Summary}
Cells make irreversible decisions---like committing to division, differentiation, or death---based on energy availability. Current mathematical models treat energy molecules like ATP simply as passive participants in reactions, missing their role as active regulatory signals that determine which pathways activate. We developed a new theoretical framework that formally distinguishes between ATP as a metabolite (consumed in reactions) and ATP as a regulatory signal (controlling pathway choice). Our theory introduces ``signal flow arcs'' that consume regulatory information during commitment, making decisions irreversible and enabling higher-level pathways to preempt lower-level ones. We demonstrate this framework by analyzing bacterial sporulation, a life-or-death decision where ATP availability determines cell fate. Our mathematical model correctly predicted the ATP threshold for commitment within 7\% of experimental values---a calculation impossible with existing models that cannot represent signal consumption. This work provides new mathematical machinery for understanding how cells make reliable decisions under changing energy conditions. The formalism has potential applications in synthetic biology (designing robust decision circuits), drug discovery (understanding ATP-dependent pathways), and precision medicine (predicting cellular responses to metabolic interventions).

\subsection{Theoretical Framework Formulation}

The framework extends classical Petri net theory with signal places (information channels) and signal flow arcs (consumption semantics). The complete formalism is specified as a 13-tuple incorporating hierarchical layer assignments for ATP-dependent regulatory control. Theoretical proofs establish layer assignment consistency for acyclic signal networks and preemption correctness guarantees.

\subsection{Analytical Method for Illustrative Application}

The \textit{Bacillus subtilis} sporulation model demonstrates the formalism's analytical capability through basin boundary computation to determine commitment thresholds from signal hierarchical structure. This analytical approach is general---similar analyses were performed for other biological systems (lambda phage lysis-lysogeny, MAPK signaling cascades), all showing results compatible with experimental data. We detail the B. subtilis case as representative example:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Hierarchical layer assignment}: Four-layer architecture from metabolic state (ATP production) through energy sensing and phosphorelay integration to execution machinery (sigma factors)
\item \textbf{Signal consumption semantics}: ATP signal flow arcs enable irreversible commitment modeling inexpressible with classical test arcs
\item \textbf{Quantitative threshold prediction}: Basin boundary computation via reachability analysis predicts ATP commitment threshold at $2.38 \pm 0.15$ mM
\item \textbf{Experimental validation}: Measured threshold of $2.21 \pm 0.18$ mM (Fujita \& Losick) yields 7\% prediction error
\end{itemize}

This case study establishes the framework's capability for computing decision thresholds through integrated metabolic-regulatory modeling with consumption semantics, demonstrating advantages over classical Bio-PN theory where such predictions are formally inexpressible.

\subsubsection{Model Parameters and Reproducibility}

Complete model specifications are documented in~\cite{subtilis2026}, including: (i) all rate constants for glycolysis, phosphorelay, and sigma factor dynamics, (ii) initial conditions for metabolic and regulatory species, (iii) signal enablement threshold values with biological justification, and (iv) comprehensive sensitivity analysis demonstrating robustness to $\pm 20\%$ parameter variations. Computational methods for basin boundary determination via reachability analysis are detailed therein, with reference implementation available in the GitHub repository.
