\hypertarget{introduction}{%
\section{3.1 Introduction}\label{introduction}}

Systems biology seeks to understand living systems through
\textbf{integration} of multiple biological scales: - Molecular
interactions (protein-protein, enzyme-substrate) - Metabolic networks
(glycolysis, TCA cycle, respiration) - Gene regulatory networks
(transcription, translation, feedback) - Signaling pathways (MAPK,
PI3K/AKT, cAMP signaling) - Cellular processes (cell cycle, apoptosis,
differentiation)

\textbf{The challenge}: These scales operate with \textbf{fundamentally
different dynamics}: - \textbf{Fast}: Enzyme kinetics (milliseconds to
seconds) - \textbf{Medium}: Metabolic fluxes (seconds to minutes) -
\textbf{Slow}: Gene expression (minutes to hours) - \textbf{Ultra-slow}:
Cell cycle (hours to days)

\textbf{Traditional approach}: Model each scale separately - Metabolic
model: ODE system for enzyme kinetics - Genetic model: Stochastic
simulation for gene expression - Signaling model: Boolean network or
ODEs

\textbf{Problem}: Real biology is \textbf{integrated} - Metabolite
concentrations regulate gene expression (e.g., glucose represses lac
operon) - Proteins regulate metabolic fluxes (e.g., allosteric feedback
inhibition) - Gene expression produces enzymes that catalyze metabolism
- \textbf{Cross-scale regulation} is pervasive, not exceptional

\textbf{This chapter}: - Presents a \textbf{motivating example}
(cAMP-CRP regulation of lac operon) demonstrating all four required
capabilities - Derives \textbf{eight formal requirements} (R1-R8) for
integrated biological modeling - Shows why \textbf{existing formalisms
fail} to meet these requirements - Motivates the \textbf{Extended
Bio-Petri Net formalism} presented in Chapters 4-6

\hypertarget{motivating-example-camp-crp-regulation-of-lac-operon}{%
\section{3.2 Motivating Example: cAMP-CRP Regulation of Lac
Operon}\label{motivating-example-camp-crp-regulation-of-lac-operon}}

\hypertarget{biological-background}{%
\subsection{3.2.1 Biological Background}\label{biological-background}}

The \textbf{lac operon} in \emph{E. coli} is a classic model of gene
regulation:

\textbf{Components}: - \textbf{lacZ gene}: Encodes β-galactosidase
(cleaves lactose \textrightarrow glucose + galactose) - \textbf{lacY gene}: Encodes
permease (imports lactose into cell) - \textbf{lacI gene}: Encodes
repressor (blocks transcription) - \textbf{Promoter}: DNA binding site
for RNA polymerase - \textbf{Operator}: DNA binding site for repressor
(overlaps promoter)

\textbf{Regulation} (simplified): 1. \textbf{No lactose}: Repressor
binds operator \textrightarrow transcription blocked 2. \textbf{Lactose present}:
Lactose binds repressor \textrightarrow repressor releases \textrightarrow transcription ON 3.
\textbf{Glucose present}: cAMP low \textrightarrow CRP inactive \textrightarrow transcription LOW
(even with lactose) 4. \textbf{No glucose}: cAMP high \textrightarrow CRP-cAMP complex
binds promoter \textrightarrow transcription HIGH

\textbf{Biological interpretation}: - \textbf{Lactose} is the
\textbf{inducer} (removes repression) - \textbf{Glucose} is the
\textbf{preferred substrate} (via cAMP-CRP catabolite repression) -
\textbf{Logic}: Use glucose first (high ATP yield), then lactose if
glucose depleted - \textbf{Cross-scale regulation}: - Metabolite
(glucose) regulates signaling (cAMP) - Signaling (cAMP-CRP) regulates
transcription (lac genes) - Transcription produces enzyme
(β-galactosidase) - Enzyme regulates metabolism (lactose \textrightarrow glucose)

\hypertarget{detailed-mechanistic-model}{%
\subsection{3.2.2 Detailed Mechanistic
Model}\label{detailed-mechanistic-model}}

\textbf{Species} (11 places): 1. \textbf{Glucose} (metabolite,
continuous concentration) 2. \textbf{Lactose} (metabolite, continuous
concentration) 3. \textbf{cAMP} (signaling molecule, continuous) 4.
\textbf{CRP} (catabolite repressor protein, continuous) 5.
\textbf{CRP-cAMP} complex (active transcription factor, continuous) 6.
\textbf{lac\_gene} (DNA, discrete count = 1) 7. \textbf{mRNA\_lac}
(messenger RNA, discrete count, stochastic) 8. \textbf{BetaGal}
(β-galactosidase enzyme, continuous) 9. \textbf{Repressor} (LacI
protein, continuous) 10. \textbf{Repressor-Lactose} complex (inactive
repressor, continuous) 11. \textbf{ATP} (energy currency, continuous)

\textbf{Transitions} (9 reactions):

\textbf{T1: Glucose consumption} (continuous, Michaelis-Menten)

\begin{lstlisting}
Glucose + ATP \textrightarrow G6P + ADP  (rate: v = V_max · [Glucose] / (K_m + [Glucose]))
\end{lstlisting}

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  Consumes glucose (normal arc)
\item
  Produces G6P (enters glycolysis)
\item
  Continuous transition (ODE integration)
\end{itemize}

\textbf{T2: cAMP synthesis} (continuous, inhibited by glucose)

\begin{lstlisting}
ATP \textrightarrow cAMP  (rate: v = k_syn / (1 + [Glucose]/K_i))
\end{lstlisting}

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{Inhibitor arc}: Glucose ⊸ T2 (threshold: K\_i = 0.5 mM)
\item
  When {[}Glucose{]} \textgreater{} K\_i: cAMP synthesis reduced
\item
  When {[}Glucose{]} \textless{} K\_i: cAMP synthesis increased
\item
  \textbf{Cross-scale coupling}: Metabolite (glucose) regulates
  signaling (cAMP)
\end{itemize}

\textbf{T3: CRP-cAMP complex formation} (continuous, mass action)

\begin{lstlisting}
CRP + cAMP ⇌ CRP-cAMP  (rate forward: k_f · [CRP] · [cAMP], reverse: k_r · [CRP-cAMP])
\end{lstlisting}

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  Reversible reaction (two transitions, or single with negative flux)
\item
  Fast equilibrium (K\_d = k\_r / k\_f ≈ 1 μM)
\end{itemize}

\textbf{T4: Repressor-Lactose binding} (continuous, fast equilibrium)

\begin{lstlisting}
Repressor + Lactose ⇌ Repressor-Lactose  (inactive repressor)
\end{lstlisting}

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  Lactose inactivates repressor (allosteric binding)
\item
  Fast equilibrium
\end{itemize}

\textbf{T5: lac operon transcription} (stochastic, burst mode)

\begin{lstlisting}
lac_gene \textrightarrow lac_gene + mRNA_lac  (burstiness: transcription in pulses)
\end{lstlisting}

\begin{itemize}
\item
  \textbf{Test arc}: lac\_gene ⤏ T5 (gene not consumed, catalytic role)
\item
  \textbf{Test arc}: CRP-cAMP ⤏ T5 (transcription factor enhances rate)
\item
  \textbf{Inhibitor arc}: Repressor ⊸ T5 (repressor blocks
  transcription)
\item
  \textbf{Stochastic transition}: Gillespie algorithm, propensity:

\begin{lstlisting}
a = k_basal + k_enhanced · [CRP-cAMP]  if [Repressor] < threshold
a = 0                                    if [Repressor] ≥ threshold
\end{lstlisting}
\item
  \textbf{Burst mode} (τ = Burst): Produces 5-10 mRNA copies per event
\item
  \textbf{Cross-scale coupling}: Signaling (cAMP-CRP) regulates genetics
  (transcription)
\end{itemize}

\textbf{T6: mRNA translation} (stochastic, discrete)

\begin{lstlisting}
mRNA_lac \textrightarrow mRNA_lac + BetaGal  (ribosome catalyzes)
\end{lstlisting}

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{Test arc}: mRNA\_lac ⤏ T6 (mRNA template not consumed during
  translation)
\item
  Stochastic transition: Each mRNA produces \textasciitilde100 proteins
  over lifetime
\item
  Propensity: a = k\_translate · {[}mRNA\_lac{]}
\end{itemize}

\textbf{T7: mRNA degradation} (stochastic, first-order)

\begin{lstlisting}
mRNA_lac \textrightarrow ∅  (RNase degrades)
\end{lstlisting}

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  Exponential lifetime: t\_half ≈ 3 minutes
\item
  Propensity: a = k\_deg · {[}mRNA\_lac{]}
\end{itemize}

\textbf{T8: Lactose import} (continuous, enzyme-catalyzed)

\begin{lstlisting}
Lactose_ext \textrightarrow Lactose  (Permease catalyzes)
\end{lstlisting}

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{Test arc}: BetaGal ⤏ T8 (enzyme not consumed)
\item
  Wait, BetaGal is β-galactosidase (cleaves lactose), not permease!
\item
  Correction: Should be \textbf{Permease} enzyme (product of lacY gene)
\item
  For simplicity, assume Permease ∝ BetaGal (both from lac operon)
\end{itemize}

\textbf{T9: Lactose cleavage} (continuous, Michaelis-Menten)

\begin{lstlisting}
Lactose \textrightarrow Glucose + Galactose  (BetaGal catalyzes)
\end{lstlisting}

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{Test arc}: BetaGal ⤏ T9 (enzyme not consumed)
\item
  \textbf{Cross-scale coupling}: Enzyme (from genetics) regulates
  metabolism
\item
  Rate: v = V\_max · {[}BetaGal{]} · {[}Lactose{]} / (K\_m +
  {[}Lactose{]})
\end{itemize}

\hypertarget{system-dynamics}{%
\subsection{3.2.3 System Dynamics}\label{system-dynamics}}

\textbf{Initial state} (t=0): - {[}Glucose{]} = 5 mM (high, preferred
substrate) - {[}Lactose{]} = 10 mM (available, but unused) - {[}cAMP{]}
= 0.1 μM (low, due to glucose) - {[}CRP-cAMP{]} ≈ 0 (low cAMP \textrightarrow no
complex) - {[}mRNA\_lac{]} = 0 (transcription repressed) - {[}BetaGal{]}
= 10 molecules (basal level)

\textbf{Phase 1: Glucose consumption} (t=0 to t=20 min) - T1 fires
continuously: Glucose \textrightarrow G6P (glycolysis) - {[}Glucose{]} decreases: 5 mM
\textrightarrow 0.1 mM - T2 (cAMP synthesis) gradually activated as glucose drops -
{[}cAMP{]} increases: 0.1 μM \textrightarrow 10 μM (100-fold) - T3: CRP + cAMP \textrightarrow
CRP-cAMP - {[}CRP-cAMP{]} increases: 0 \textrightarrow 5 μM

\textbf{Phase 2: Lac operon induction} (t=20 to t=40 min) -
{[}CRP-cAMP{]} high \textrightarrow T5 (transcription) activated - T5 fires
stochastically: Bursts of 5-10 mRNA copies - {[}mRNA\_lac{]} increases:
0 \textrightarrow 50 molecules (stochastic fluctuations) - T6 fires stochastically:
mRNA \textrightarrow BetaGal - {[}BetaGal{]} increases: 10 \textrightarrow 5000 molecules (500-fold
induction)

\textbf{Phase 3: Lactose utilization} (t=40 to t=80 min) - {[}BetaGal{]}
high \textrightarrow T9 activated - T9 fires continuously: Lactose \textrightarrow Glucose +
Galactose - {[}Lactose{]} decreases: 10 mM \textrightarrow 0 mM - {[}Glucose{]}
increases: 0.1 mM \textrightarrow 5 mM (replenished from lactose) - \textbf{Feedback}:
Glucose rise \textrightarrow {[}cAMP{]} drops \textrightarrow transcription reduced - System returns
to low basal state

\textbf{Steady state} (t \textgreater{} 80 min): - {[}Glucose{]} = 5 mM
(maintained by lactose cleavage) - {[}Lactose{]} = 0 mM (depleted) -
{[}BetaGal{]} = 5000 molecules (stable, long protein half-life) -
{[}mRNA\_lac{]} = 5 molecules (basal level, transcription off)

\hypertarget{four-capabilities-required}{%
\subsection{3.2.4 Four Capabilities
Required}\label{four-capabilities-required}}

\textbf{Capability 1: Weak independence \& cooperative parallelism}

Multiple transitions share places but remain parallelizable: -
\textbf{T2 (cAMP synthesis)} and \textbf{T3 (CRP-cAMP formation)} both
interact with cAMP place - T2 produces cAMP (output) - T3 consumes cAMP
(input) - But T3 also produces cAMP (reversible reaction, back-reaction)
- Shared place: cAMP - Weak independence: No (T3 consumes T2's product \textrightarrow
sequential dependency)

Better example: - \textbf{T5 (transcription)} and \textbf{T6
(translation)} both interact with mRNA\_lac - T5 produces mRNA (output)
- T6 uses mRNA as template (test arc, non-consumptive) - \textbf{Weak
independence}: YES (T6 does not consume mRNA, test arc) - Can execute in
parallel (superposition: multiple ribosomes translate same mRNA)

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{T9 (lactose cleavage)} produces Glucose
\item
  \textbf{T1 (glucose consumption)} consumes Glucose
\item
  Shared place: Glucose (convergent flux)
\item
  Weak independence: Only if T9 produces to Glucose and T1 consumes from
  Glucose (disjoint input sets for each)
\item
  Actually, T1 consumes Glucose (input), T9 produces Glucose (output) \textrightarrow
  NOT weakly independent (T1 consumes T9's product)
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Clearer example for weak independence}: - Suppose we have
\textbf{two pathways producing ATP}: - Glycolysis: Glucose \textrightarrow ATP (via
T1') - Oxidative phosphorylation: O2 \textrightarrow ATP (via T\_oxphos) - Both
produce ATP (shared output) - Disjoint inputs: Glucose ≠ O2 -
\textbf{Weakly independent}: Can compute both fluxes in parallel,
effects add to ATP pool

\textbf{Capability 2: Heterogeneous transition types coexistence}

The lac operon model requires \textbf{four transition types} in a single
network: - \textbf{Continuous}: T1 (glucose consumption), T2 (cAMP
synthesis), T3 (complex formation), T9 (lactose cleavage) - ODE
integration: dM/dt = Φ(M) - Fast equilibrium reactions (T3) use stiff
ODE solvers - \textbf{Stochastic}: T5 (transcription), T6 (translation),
T7 (mRNA degradation) - Gillespie algorithm: Discrete events,
exponentially distributed intervals - Low copy numbers (mRNA: 0-50
molecules) - \textbf{Burst}: T5 specifically uses \textbf{burst mode} -
Transcription occurs in pulses (RNA polymerase processivity) - Each
burst produces 5-10 mRNA copies (geometric distribution) - Inter-burst
intervals: Exponential with rate λ\_burst - \textbf{Timed}: Not used in
this example, but could model: - Cell cycle checkpoints (G1 \textrightarrow S
transition after fixed delay) - Scheduled degradation (programmed
protein turnover)

\textbf{Challenge}: Synchronize all four types - Continuous ODE step: Δt
= 0.01 s (adaptive) - Stochastic Gillespie: τ\_next = -ln(rand()) /
a\_total (event-driven) - Burst events: Independent exponential process
- \textbf{Hybrid scheduler} required (see Chapter 11)

\textbf{Capability 3: Arc-level regulation with biochemical semantics}

Regulatory logic is \textbf{embedded in the topology} (not hidden in
code):

\textbf{Test arcs} (non-consumptive catalysis): -
\passthrough{\lstinline!lac\_gene ⤏ T5!}: Gene is template for
transcription (not consumed) - \passthrough{\lstinline!CRP-cAMP ⤏ T5!}:
Transcription factor enhances rate (not consumed) -
\passthrough{\lstinline!mRNA\_lac ⤏ T6!}: mRNA is template for
translation (not consumed) - \passthrough{\lstinline!BetaGal ⤏ T9!}:
Enzyme catalyzes reaction (not consumed)

\textbf{Inhibitor arcs} (threshold-based blocking): -
\passthrough{\lstinline!Glucose ⊸ T2!}: High glucose inhibits cAMP
synthesis - Threshold formula: Δ(Glucose, T2) = K\_i = 0.5 mM - Enabling
condition: {[}Glucose{]} \textless{} 0.5 mM -
\passthrough{\lstinline!Repressor ⊸ T5!}: Repressor blocks transcription
- Threshold: Δ(Repressor, T5) = 10 nM - Enabling condition:
{[}Repressor{]} \textless{} 10 nM

\textbf{Hill equation} (cooperative regulation): - Could use Hill
equation for CRP-cAMP activation of T5: - Δ(CRP-cAMP, T5) = K\^{}n /
(K\^{}n + {[}CRP-cAMP{]}\^{}n) - Cooperative binding (n=2 for CRP dimer)

\textbf{Visual representation}:

\begin{lstlisting}
         Glucose
            |
            ⊸ (inhibitor, blocks when high)
            |
         [T2: cAMP_synthesis]
            |
            \textrightarrow cAMP \textrightarrow [T3] \textrightarrow CRP-cAMP
                                |
                                ⤏ (test arc, activates)
                                |
    lac_gene ⤏ [T5: Transcription] \textleftarrow ⊸ Repressor
                                |
                                \textrightarrow mRNA_lac
                                    |
                                    ⤏ (test arc, template)
                                    |
                                 [T6: Translation]
                                    |
                                    \textrightarrow BetaGal
                                        |
                                        ⤏ (test arc, catalyst)
                                        |
                                     [T9: Lactose_cleavage]
                                        |
                                        \textrightarrow Glucose (feedback loop!)
\end{lstlisting}

\textbf{Key insight}: The entire regulatory logic is visible in the
\textbf{network topology}. An external viewer can understand: - Glucose
inhibits cAMP synthesis (inhibitor arc) - CRP-cAMP activates
transcription (test arc) - Repressor blocks transcription (inhibitor
arc) - BetaGal catalyzes lactose cleavage (test arc) - Glucose
production feeds back to cAMP inhibition (cycle)

tracking}

Each place has \textbf{elemental composition}: - \textbf{Glucose}:
C₆H₁₂O₆ - \textbf{Lactose}: C₁₂H₂₂O₁₁ (disaccharide: Glucose + Galactose

\hypertarget{summary-of-requirements}{%
\subsection{3.2.5 Summary of
Requirements}\label{summary-of-requirements}}

The lac operon example demonstrates the need for: 1. \checkmark \textbf{Weak
independence}: mRNA template shared via test arc (parallel translation)
2. \checkmark \textbf{Heterogeneous transitions}: Continuous (metabolism) +
Stochastic (transcription) + Burst (mRNA bursts) 3. \checkmark \textbf{Arc-level
regulation}: Glucose ⊸ cAMP synthesis, CRP-cAMP ⤏ transcription,
Repressor ⊸ transcription 4. \checkmark \textbf{Atomic conservation}: Glucose
(C₆H₁₂O₆), ATP (C₁₀H₁₆N₅O₁₃P₃), elemental balance verified

\hypertarget{eight-formal-requirements-for-integrated-biological-modeling}{%
\section{3.3 Eight Formal Requirements for Integrated Biological
Modeling}\label{eight-formal-requirements-for-integrated-biological-modeling}}

Based on the lac operon example and broader analysis of systems biology
models, we derive \textbf{eight formal requirements} (R1-R8):

\hypertarget{r1-multi-scale-temporal-dynamics-milliseconds-to-hours}{%
\subsection{\texorpdfstring{\textbf{R1: Multi-scale temporal dynamics}
(milliseconds to
hours)}{R1: Multi-scale temporal dynamics (milliseconds to hours)}}\label{r1-multi-scale-temporal-dynamics-milliseconds-to-hours}}

\textbf{Requirement}: Formalism must support processes spanning 6+
orders of magnitude in timescale.

\textbf{Justification}: - Enzyme kinetics: 10⁻³ - 10⁰ seconds
(milliseconds to seconds) - Metabolic steady state: 10⁰ - 10² seconds
(seconds to minutes) - Gene expression: 10² - 10⁴ seconds (minutes to
hours) - Cell cycle: 10⁴ - 10⁵ seconds (hours to days)

\textbf{Example} (lac operon): - cAMP-CRP binding: t\_equilibrium ≈ 0.1
s (fast) - mRNA half-life: t\_half ≈ 180 s (3 minutes) - Lac operon
induction: t\_response ≈ 1200 s (20 minutes)

\textbf{Implication}: Single timescale models (e.g., fixed Δt ODE)
cannot capture both fast equilibria and slow gene expression.

\textbf{Solution}: Heterogeneous transition types (Continuous for fast,
Stochastic for slow, adaptive time steps).

\begin{center}\rule{0.5\linewidth}{0.5pt}\end{center}

\hypertarget{r2-discrete-and-continuous-state-variables-hybrid-semantics}{%
\subsection{\texorpdfstring{\textbf{R2: Discrete and continuous state
variables} (hybrid
semantics)}{R2: Discrete and continuous state variables (hybrid semantics)}}\label{r2-discrete-and-continuous-state-variables-hybrid-semantics}}

\textbf{Requirement}: Formalism must support both discrete counts
(genes, mRNA copies) and continuous concentrations (metabolites,
proteins).

\textbf{Justification}: - Genes: Discrete (1 copy per cell for bacterial
chromosome, 2 copies for diploid) - mRNA: Discrete (0-100 copies,
stochastic fluctuations) - Proteins: Continuous (1000-1,000,000 copies,
law of large numbers applies) - Metabolites: Continuous (millimolar
concentrations, millions of molecules)

\textbf{Example} (lac operon): - lac\_gene: Discrete (1 copy) -
mRNA\_lac: Discrete (0-50 copies, Poisson-like distribution) - BetaGal:
Continuous (10-10,000 molecules, treat as concentration) - Glucose:
Continuous (0-10 mM = 10¹⁶ molecules per cell, deterministic)

\textbf{Implication}: Pure discrete models (e.g., Gillespie for entire
network) are computationally intractable for high-copy metabolites. Pure
continuous models (ODEs) miss stochastic gene expression noise.

\textbf{Solution}: Hybrid semantics (discrete marking for low-copy,
continuous marking for high-copy).

\begin{center}\rule{0.5\linewidth}{0.5pt}\end{center}

\hypertarget{r3-non-consumptive-participation-catalysis-templating}{%
\subsection{\texorpdfstring{\textbf{R3: Non-consumptive participation}
(catalysis,
templating)}{R3: Non-consumptive participation (catalysis, templating)}}\label{r3-non-consumptive-participation-catalysis-templating}}

\textbf{Requirement}: Formalism must distinguish between consumptive
reactions (substrates) and non-consumptive roles (enzymes, genes,
transcription factors).

\textbf{Justification}: - Enzymes: Participate in reactions but are not
consumed (k\_cat turnover, enzyme released) - Genes: Serve as templates
for transcription (DNA not depleted) - mRNA: Templates for translation
(mRNA produces many proteins before degradation) - Transcription
factors: Bind DNA, enhance transcription, but are not degraded

\textbf{Example} (lac operon): - lac\_gene ⤏ Transcription: Gene is
template (test arc, non-consumptive) - BetaGal ⤏ Lactose\_cleavage:
Enzyme catalyzes (test arc, non-consumptive) - If modeled with normal
arcs: Gene and enzyme would deplete after single firing \texttimes

\textbf{Implication}: Classical Petri nets (only normal arcs) cannot
model catalysis without artificial workarounds (e.g., enzyme \textrightarrow enzyme +
product, doubling token count).

\textbf{Solution}: Test arcs (p ⤏ t) with semantics: M'(p) = M(p)
(unchanged after firing).

\begin{center}\rule{0.5\linewidth}{0.5pt}\end{center}

\hypertarget{r4-threshold-based-regulation-inhibition-activation-thresholds}{%
\subsection{\texorpdfstring{\textbf{R4: Threshold-based regulation}
(inhibition, activation
thresholds)}{R4: Threshold-based regulation (inhibition, activation thresholds)}}\label{r4-threshold-based-regulation-inhibition-activation-thresholds}}

\textbf{Requirement}: Formalism must support enabling conditions based
on \textbf{place markings exceeding or falling below thresholds}.

\textbf{Justification}: - Allosteric inhibition: Product inhibits enzyme
when {[}Product{]} \textgreater{} K\_i (e.g., ATP inhibits PFK) -
Transcriptional repression: Repressor blocks transcription when
{[}Repressor{]} \textgreater{} threshold - Ultrasensitivity: Sigmoidal
response curves (Hill equation) require threshold-like behavior

\textbf{Example} (lac operon): - Glucose ⊸ cAMP\_synthesis: Transition
blocked when {[}Glucose{]} \textgreater{} 0.5 mM - Repressor ⊸
Transcription: Transition blocked when {[}Repressor{]} \textgreater{} 10
nM - CRP-cAMP ⤏ Transcription: Rate enhanced when {[}CRP-cAMP{]}
\textgreater{} threshold (could use Hill equation)

\textbf{Implication}: Classical Petri nets have binary enabling (M(p) ≥
W(p,t) or not). Cannot express ``fire only if M(p) \textless{}
threshold''.

\textbf{Solution}: Inhibitor arcs (p ⊸ t) with threshold functions
Δ(p,t). Enabling condition: M(p) \textless{} Δ(p,t).

\begin{center}\rule{0.5\linewidth}{0.5pt}\end{center}

\hypertarget{r5-cooperative-and-convergent-processes-shared-outputs-superposition}{%
\subsection{\texorpdfstring{\textbf{R5: Cooperative and convergent
processes} (shared outputs,
superposition)}{R5: Cooperative and convergent processes (shared outputs, superposition)}}\label{r5-cooperative-and-convergent-processes-shared-outputs-superposition}}

\textbf{Requirement}: Formalism must allow multiple transitions to
\textbf{produce to the same place} without conflict (additive effects).

\textbf{Justification}: - Convergent metabolism: Multiple pathways
produce same metabolite (e.g., pyruvate from glycolysis, lactate,
alanine) - Parallel enzyme activity: Multiple enzyme copies act on same
substrate pool (effects add) - Multiple transcription factors: Several
activators enhance same promoter (additive or synergistic)

\textbf{Example} (lac operon): - Lactose cleavage produces Glucose -
Glycolysis consumes Glucose - But also: External glucose import produces
Glucose - Two sources (lactose cleavage + import) \textrightarrow single Glucose pool
(convergent)

\textbf{More general example} (pyruvate):

\begin{lstlisting}
Glycolysis \textrightarrow Pyruvate (flux v₁)
Lactate_oxidation \textrightarrow Pyruvate (flux v₂)
Alanine_transamination \textrightarrow Pyruvate (flux v₃)
Net: dM(Pyruvate)/dt = v₁ + v₂ + v₃ (superposition)
\end{lstlisting}

\textbf{Implication}: Classical strong independence rejects transitions
with shared outputs (conflict condition). But biologically, shared
outputs are \textbf{cooperative}, not conflicting.

\textbf{Solution}: Weak independence (disjoint inputs, shared outputs
allowed). Parallel execution with synchronized accumulation.

\begin{center}\rule{0.5\linewidth}{0.5pt}\end{center}

\hypertarget{r6-multi-type-kinetics-mass-action-michaelis-menten-hill-stochastic-propensities}{%
\subsection{\texorpdfstring{\textbf{R6: Multi-type kinetics} (mass
action, Michaelis-Menten, Hill, stochastic
propensities)}{R6: Multi-type kinetics (mass action, Michaelis-Menten, Hill, stochastic propensities)}}\label{r6-multi-type-kinetics-mass-action-michaelis-menten-hill-stochastic-propensities}}

\textbf{Requirement}: Formalism must support diverse rate functions
within a single model.

\textbf{Justification}: - Fast binding: Mass action (k · {[}A{]} ·
{[}B{]}) - Enzyme kinetics: Michaelis-Menten (V\_max · {[}S{]} / (K\_m +
{[}S{]})) - Cooperative regulation: Hill equation (V\_max · {[}S{]}ⁿ /
(K\^{}n + {[}S{]}ⁿ)) - Stochastic gene expression: Propensity functions
(a = k\_basal + k\_induced · {[}TF{]}) - Bursts: Geometric distribution
for burst size, exponential inter-burst intervals

\textbf{Example} (lac operon): - T1 (glucose consumption):
Michaelis-Menten (saturable enzyme kinetics) - T2 (cAMP synthesis):
Inhibited synthesis (k / (1 + {[}Glucose{]}/K\_i)) - T3 (complex
formation): Mass action (k\_f · {[}CRP{]} · {[}cAMP{]}) - T5
(transcription): Stochastic propensity + burst mode - T9 (lactose
cleavage): Michaelis-Menten with enzyme term (V\_max · {[}E{]} · {[}S{]}
/ (K\_m + {[}S{]}))

\textbf{Implication}: Formalism must provide \textbf{rate function
library} Φ and allow \textbf{per-transition customization}.

\textbf{Solution}: Rate function map Φ: T \textrightarrow RateFunction. Each
transition has type τ(t) and associated kinetic parameters.

\begin{center}\rule{0.5\linewidth}{0.5pt}\end{center}

\hypertarget{r7-elemental-conservation-mass-balance-at-atomic-level}{%
\subsection{\texorpdfstring{\textbf{R7: Elemental conservation} (mass
balance at atomic
level)}{R7: Elemental conservation (mass balance at atomic level)}}\label{r7-elemental-conservation-mass-balance-at-atomic-level}}

\textbf{Requirement}: Formalism must track \textbf{elemental
composition} (C, H, O, N, P, S) and verify conservation laws.

\textbf{Justification}: - Fundamental physics: Atoms are neither created
nor destroyed (non-nuclear biochemistry) - Debugging: Elemental
imbalance indicates modeling error (missing cofactor, wrong
stoichiometry) - Redox balance: Electron conservation in
oxidation-reduction reactions - ATP budget: Phosphate balance in energy
metabolism

\textbf{Example} (lac operon): - Lactose (C₁₂H₂₂O₁₁) \textrightarrow Glucose (C₆H₁₂O₆)
+ Galactose (C₆H₁₂O₆) - Check: 12 = 6+6 (C), 22+2 = 12+12 (H), 11+1 =
6+6 (O) \checkmark - If forgot water: 12=12 (C) \checkmark, but 22 ≠ 24 (H) \texttimes \textrightarrow Error
detected

\textbf{Implication}: Classical Petri nets use abstract tokens (no
chemical identity). Cannot verify conservation.

\textbf{Solution}: Biochemical formula map ρ: T \textrightarrow Formula. Elemental
balance matrix S\_e. Verify: S\_e · v = 0 (steady state).

\hypertarget{r8-parallelism-for-computational-efficiency-simulation-performance}{%
\subsection{\texorpdfstring{\textbf{R8: Parallelism for computational
efficiency} (simulation
performance)}{R8: Parallelism for computational efficiency (simulation performance)}}\label{r8-parallelism-for-computational-efficiency-simulation-performance}}

\textbf{Requirement}: Formalism must enable \textbf{parallel execution}
of independent processes to achieve scalable performance.

\textbf{Justification}: - Large models: Genome-scale metabolism (1000+
reactions), whole-cell models (5000+ processes) - Real-time simulation:
Parameter sweeps (100+ conditions), sensitivity analysis (1000+ samples)
- Multi-core hardware: Modern CPUs (8-64 cores), GPUs (1000+ cores)

\textbf{Example} (lac operon): - Sequential simulation: 9 transitions ×
τ = 9τ per time step - Parallel (2 cores): - Set 1: \{T1, T5\} (glucose
consumption + transcription, independent) - Set 2: \{T2, T6\} (cAMP
synthesis + translation, independent) - Set 3: \{T3, T9\} (complex
formation + lactose cleavage) - Set 4: \{T4, T7\} (repressor binding +
mRNA degradation) - Total: 5 sequential sets \textrightarrow 5τ (speedup: 9/5 = 1.8×)

\textbf{Better example} (genome-scale): - 1000 transitions, 650 weakly
independent pairs - Classical strong independence: 200 pairs \textrightarrow 5
parallel sets (speedup: 5×) - Weak independence: 650 pairs \textrightarrow 3 parallel
sets (speedup: 10×) - \textbf{Result}: 2× speedup improvement by
recognizing biological cooperativity

\textbf{Implication}: Formalism must provide \textbf{dependency
analysis} (which transitions conflict) and \textbf{parallel execution
semantics} (safe concurrent firing).

\textbf{Solution}: Weak independence theory (Chapter 5). Dependency
classification algorithm. Parallel scheduler.

\hypertarget{the-integration-gap}{%
\section{3.5 The Integration Gap}\label{the-integration-gap}}

\hypertarget{current-practice-separate-models}{%
\subsection{3.5.1 Current Practice: Separate
Models}\label{current-practice-separate-models}}

\textbf{Standard approach} in systems biology: 1. \textbf{Metabolic
model}: SBML ODE system (continuous, deterministic) 2. \textbf{Gene
regulatory model}: Boolean network or stochastic simulation (discrete,
probabilistic) 3. \textbf{Signaling model}: ODE system or rule-based
(Kappa)

\textbf{Integration attempt}: - Run metabolic model \textrightarrow compute
{[}Glucose{]} - Use {[}Glucose{]} as input to gene regulatory model \textrightarrow
compute {[}mRNA\_lac{]} - Use {[}mRNA\_lac{]} to update enzyme levels in
metabolic model \textrightarrow update {[}BetaGal{]} - \textbf{Problem}: Loose
coupling, manual synchronization, no formal semantics

\textbf{Example} (lac operon): - \textbf{File 1}:
\passthrough{\lstinline!metabolism.xml!} (SBML) - Species: Glucose,
Lactose, G6P, ATP, ADP - Reactions: Hexokinase, PFK, Lactose\_cleavage -
Enzyme parameters: V\_max = f({[}BetaGal{]}) (imported from File 2)

\begin{itemize}
\item
  \textbf{File 2}: \passthrough{\lstinline!gene\_regulation.py!}
  (Gillespie script)

  \begin{itemize}
  \tightlist
  \item
    Species: lac\_gene, mRNA\_lac, BetaGal
  \item
    Reactions: Transcription, Translation, Degradation
  \item
    Regulation: Transcription rate = f({[}cAMP-CRP{]}, {[}Glucose{]})
    (imported from File 1)
  \end{itemize}
\item
  \textbf{File 3}: \passthrough{\lstinline!integrate.py!} (custom
  script)

\begin{lstlisting}[language=Python]
for t in time_steps:
    # Step 1: Run metabolism (SBML)
    glucose, lactose = run_metabolism_ode(t, dt, beta_gal)

    # Step 2: Run gene regulation (Gillespie)
    mrna_lac, beta_gal = run_gillespie(t, dt, glucose, lactose)

    # Step 3: Synchronize (manual)
    # Update BetaGal in metabolism model
    # Update Glucose in gene regulation model
\end{lstlisting}
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Problems}: 1. \textbf{Manual synchronization}: Requires custom
glue code for every model pair 2. \textbf{No formal semantics}: When to
synchronize? What if Gillespie event occurs mid-ODE step? 3.
\textbf{Error-prone}: Easy to miss feedback loops (e.g., Glucose \textrightarrow cAMP
\textrightarrow Transcription \textrightarrow BetaGal \textrightarrow Lactose \textrightarrow Glucose) 4. \textbf{Not reusable}:
Custom integration script per model 5. \textbf{No topological
visibility}: Regulation hidden in code, not visible in network

\hypertarget{the-need-for-unified-formalism}{%
\subsection{3.5.2 The Need for Unified
Formalism}\label{the-need-for-unified-formalism}}

\textbf{Goal}: Single model representation capturing all scales and
semantics.

\textbf{Requirements for unified formalism}: 1. \textbf{Single network}:
All species (genes, mRNA, proteins, metabolites) as nodes 2.
\textbf{Hybrid dynamics}: Discrete (genes, mRNA) + continuous
(metabolites) 3. \textbf{Heterogeneous transitions}: Continuous
(metabolism) + stochastic (gene expression) + burst (transcription) 4.
\textbf{Topological regulation}: Inhibitor/test arcs visible in network
5. \textbf{Automatic synchronization}: Built-in hybrid scheduler 6.
\textbf{Elemental conservation}: Automatic verification

\textbf{Extended Bio-PN achieves this} (see Chapters 4-6).

  \begin{itemize}

  \item

  \item

  \item
    R3: Non-consumptive participation (catalysis)
  \item
    R4: Threshold-based regulation (inhibition)
  \item
    R5: Cooperative and convergent processes (shared outputs)
  \item
    R6: Multi-type kinetics (Michaelis-Menten, Hill, stochastic)
  \item
    R7: Elemental conservation (mass balance)
  \item
    R8: Parallelism for computational efficiency
  \end{itemize}
\item
  \textbf{Existing formalisms fail}:

  \begin{itemize}
  \tightlist
  \item

    semantics (1/8 requirements)
  \item

    requirements)
  \item
    Hybrid Petri nets: Close, but lack test/inhibitor arcs and
    biochemical formulas (4/8 requirements)
  \item
    Rule-based models: Good for combinatorial complexity, but sequential
    and discrete-only (2/8 requirements)
  \end{itemize}
\item
  \textbf{Integration gap}: Current practice uses separate models with
  manual coupling. Need \textbf{unified formalism}.
\end{enumerate}
